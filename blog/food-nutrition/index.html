Section 1

THE HUMAN NUTRIENT BLUEPRINT

The human body requires two foundational tiers of nutrients to generate energy, build immunity, and regulate hormone production:

🥩 1. Protein (Building Blocks)

  • Tissue Repair: Rebuilds muscle fibers, skin, and organs.
  • Biochemical Defense: Synthesizes antibodies and metabolic enzymes.
  • Neurotransmitters: Powers dopamine and serotonin production.
  • High Satiety: Keeps you full longer and suppresses hunger spikes.
🎯 Target: 1.2 – 1.6g / kg bodyweight

🌾 2. Complex Carbs & Fiber

  • Glycogen Storage: Primary glucose fuel for brain and skeletal muscles.
  • Prebiotic Soluble Fiber: Nourishes healthy gut microbiome bacteria.
  • Insoluble Fiber: Prevents constipation and controls blood cholesterol.
  • Sustained Energy: Prevents sudden post-meal insulin crashes.
🎯 Target: 45–55% energy | 30g+ Fiber

🥑 3. Essential Fats (MUFA / PUFA)

  • Steroid Hormones: Essential for testosterone, estrogen, and cortisol.
  • Cell Membranes: Maintains phospholipid fluidity in every human cell.
  • Brain Density: 60% of the brain is structural fat.
  • Vitamin Vehicle: Required to absorb Vitamins A, D, E, and K.
🎯 Target: 20–30% energy | Focus on Omega-3

💧 4. Water & Electrolytes

  • Blood Volume: Ensures smooth oxygen transport across vital organs.
  • Osmotic Balance: Regulated by Sodium, Potassium, and Magnesium.
  • Joint Lubrication: Protects synovial fluid in joints and spine.
  • Brain Function: A 2% drop in hydration causes brain fog and fatigue.
🎯 Target: 2.5 – 3.5 Litres / day

☀️ 5. Fat-Soluble Vitamins (A, D, E, K)

  • Vitamin D3: Regulates 1,000+ genes, bone density, and mood.
  • Vitamin A: Preserves night vision and epithelial skin lining.
  • Vitamin E: Shields cell membranes against oxidative damage.
  • Vitamin K2: Directs calcium into bones and away from arteries.
⚠️ 85%+ Urban Indians lack Vitamin D3

⚡ 6. Water-Soluble Vitamins (B & C)

  • B-Complex (B1–B9): Converts food carbohydrates and fats into ATP energy.
  • Vitamin B12: Critical for nerve sheath integrity and RBC formation.
  • Vitamin C: Crucial for collagen synthesis and antioxidant defense.
  • Iron Booster: Triples absorption of plant-based non-heme iron.
⚠️ Vitamin B12 is strictly non-plant

⚙️ 7. Key Minerals (Mg, Fe, Ca, Zn)

  • Magnesium: Regulates 300+ enzymes, relaxes muscles, aids deep sleep.
  • Iron: Transports oxygen via hemoglobin to prevent chronic fatigue.
  • Calcium: Provides skeletal strength and neuromuscular signaling.
  • Zinc: Accelerates wound healing and supports immune T-cells.
💡 Always pair Plant Iron with Vitamin C

🦠 8. Gut Microbiome / Probiotics

  • Immune Headquarters: Over 70% of human immune cells reside in the gut.
  • SCFA Production: Ferments fiber into butyrate to protect intestinal walls.
  • Gut-Brain Axis: 90% of bodily serotonin is generated in the digestive tract.
  • Daily Staples: Fresh homemade curd and spiced buttermilk (majjige).
🥛 Probiotic Staple: Daily Curd / Majjige

TOP 8 NUTRITION MYTHS BUSTED

Common myths shared across social media and office WhatsApp groups that sabotage everyday health:

Myth 1 "Carbs make you fat — eliminate rice and rotis completely."
  • Caloric Surplus: Fat gain is driven by chronic excess calories, not carbohydrates.
  • Brain & Muscle Fuel: Complex carbs (millets, red rice, oats) provide sustained energy and vital prebiotic fiber.
  • The Real Culprits: Ultra-processed refined carbs like bakery maida, sugary beverages, and deep-fried snacks.
Myth 2 "Indian vegetarian food cannot provide sufficient protein."
  • Complementary Proteins: Classic pairings like Dal + Chawal or Rajma + Rice provide complete amino acid profiles.
  • Power Staples: Soya chunks contain 52% protein by weight — higher than any meat or poultry.
  • Easy Targets: Adding paneer, tofu, sattu, and Greek yogurt makes hitting 80–100g daily protein seamless without powders.
Myth 3 "Desi Ghee clogs arteries and leads straight to heart attacks."
  • Gut Healing: Pure desi ghee is loaded with butyric acid, which repairs and nourishes the intestinal lining.
  • Thermal Stability: High smoke point prevents toxic lipid peroxidation during Indian cooking.
  • The Real Hazard: Repeatedly reheated commercial palm oils and trans-fat shortenings used in street frying.
Myth 4 "Egg yolks are bad for cholesterol; eat only egg whites."
  • Minimal Blood Impact: Dietary cholesterol does not meaningfully raise blood LDL in 75%+ of the population.
  • Nutrient Reservoir: Yolks contain 90% of the egg's micronutrients: choline (for brain health), Vitamins A, D, and B12.
  • Optimal Dose: 1–2 whole eggs daily supports cardiovascular and neurological health.
Myth 5 "Jaggery (Gur) and Honey are healthy, guilt-free sugar replacements."
  • Identical Sugar Content: Jaggery is ~90% sucrose/glucose, and honey is ~80% fructose/glucose.
  • Glycemic Impact: Both trigger the exact same insulin spike and visceral fat accumulation as white table sugar.
  • The Takeaway: Enjoy them for authentic flavor, but never treat them as weight-loss foods.
Myth 6 "Detox green juices and teas flush out bodily toxins."
  • Natural Detox Organs: Your liver, kidneys, lungs, and skin detoxify your body around the clock.
  • Juicing Downsides: Juicing removes essential fiber, leaving concentrated liquid fructose that stresses the liver.
  • Better Choice: Eat whole raw fruits and steamed vegetables with intact natural fiber.
Myth 7 "You must drink 4–5 liters of water daily, preferably alkaline water."
  • Stomach Acid Reality: Stomach acid has a pH of 1.5–2.0; alkaline water is neutralized immediately upon swallowing.
  • Hyponatremia Risk: Guzzling excessive plain water flushes out sodium and potassium, causing brain fog and muscle cramps.
  • Optimal Rule: 2.5–3.5 liters of filtered mineral-rich water is ideal for most urban adults.
Myth 8 "Eating food after 8 PM instantly turns into stored body fat."
  • Metabolic Clock: Total 24-hour calorie intake dictates fat gain, not the time displayed on the clock.
  • Sleep Impact: Heavy dinners eaten right before bed do disrupt REM sleep and provoke acid reflux.
  • Golden Rule: Finish your last meal 2 hours before sleeping for restorative rest.

  • The Problem: Deep-fried bajjis and sugary filter coffee spark a 5:30 PM insulin crash and brain fog.
  • Smart Swap 1: Spiced Majjige (buttermilk with curry leaves, crushed ginger, and rock salt) — gut cooling and probiotic.

⚡ Zepto / Blinkit 10-Minute Cart Upgrades

  • Remove from Cart: Biscuits, processed mixtures, and instant cup noodles.

Section 6

PRACTICAL DOS & DON'TS

✅ THE DO'S

✔
Frontload Protein at Breakfast: Start with eggs, paneer bhurji, or sprouted moong cheela to extinguish 11 AM sugar cravings.
✔
Squeeze Lemon on Meals: Vitamin C triples plant-based non-heme iron absorption from dals and leafy greens.
✔
Eat Greens & Salad First: Consuming fiber before rice or rotis slows gastric emptying and reduces post-meal glucose spikes by up to 35%.
✔
Hydrate with Minerals: Sip tender coconut water or majjige with rock salt rather than chugging plain filtered water mindlessly.
✔
Walk 10 Mins After Meals: A short brisk post-meal stroll activates muscle GLUT4 receptors to soak up blood glucose without insulin spikes.

❌ THE DON'TS

✖
Don't Drink Chai/Coffee with Meals: Tannins and polyphenols bind to dietary iron and block up to 70% of absorption. Keep a 1-hour gap!
✖
Don't Reheat Cooking Oils: Reheating oils beyond smoke point creates carcinogenic trans-fats and lipid peroxides. Discard used frying oil.
✖
Don't Drink Your Fruit: Packaged juices and juice stalls remove all dietary fiber, dumping concentrated liquid fructose straight into your liver.
✖
Don't Fall for "Zero Sugar" Jaggery Snacks: Jaggery sweets spike blood glucose and insulin almost just as sharply as white table sugar.
✖
Don't Rely Solely on Plain Dal for Protein: A standard bowl of watery dal contains only 3–5g protein and 20g carbs. Add paneer, soya, or curd to balance it.

THE HUMAN BASELINE & CUSTOM CALCULATOR

Nutrient requirements are not arbitrary numbers. They are calibrated against official medical reference standards (ICMR-National Institute of Nutrition 2020 & WHO):

Standard Reference Human (ICMR-NIN & WHO Baseline):

  • Reference Adult Male: Body Weight: 65 kg (143 lbs) | Height: 172 cm (5'8") | BMI: 22.0
  • Reference Adult Female: Body Weight: 55 kg (121 lbs) | Height: 160 cm (5'3") | BMI: 21.5
  • Proportional Scaling Rule:
    • Protein: Scales at 0.8 – 1.0g / kg for sedentary individuals, and 1.2 – 1.6g / kg for active adults/weightlifters. If you weigh 80 kg, your target scales up to 96–128g; if you weigh 50 kg, it scales down to 60–80g.
    • Water: Scales at 35 ml per kg of body weight (+500ml for heavy sweating or workout sessions).
    • Fiber: Scales with total caloric turnover (~14g per 1,000 kcal consumed; baseline 30–38g for men, 25–30g for women).
    • Micronutrients (Vitamins & Minerals): Remain largely constant based on biological sex and organ volume (e.g. Iron is higher for menstruating women: 29 mg vs 19 mg in men; Calcium is 1000 mg across all adults).
